# MTA Ridership Takeaways

All charts are normalized to each group's own peak, so shapes show *when* people ride, not *how many*.

## Fair Fare

Metrocard and OMNY riders have nearly identical daily rhythms across all boroughs: a morning commute bump, a stronger evening peak, and quiet overnight hours. Full-fare riders behave the same regardless of payment method.

## Seniors & Disability

The most interesting divergence. Metrocard seniors show a rounder, midday-heavy pattern — riding throughout the day with a broad peak around 10am–3pm. OMNY seniors show sharper morning/evening commute peaks. This suggests OMNY-adopting seniors may skew younger or more commute-oriented, while Metrocard holdouts ride more leisurely, off-peak schedules.

## Students

Ridership is heavily concentrated around 7–8am and 2–4pm, mapping to school start and end times. Virtually zero overnight/late-night ridership. Bronx and Brooklyn show an especially sharp morning spike.

## Cross-Borough

Shapes are remarkably consistent across boroughs — time-of-day behavior is driven by rider *type* (commuter, senior, student) more than by *geography*. The subway clock is set by the social roles of its riders.

---

## OMNY Is Eating Metrocard

OMNY's share of total ridership climbed from ~20% in early 2022 to ~60% by late 2024. The crossover point — when OMNY overtook Metrocard in monthly volume — landed around mid-2023. Total ridership has grown too, so Metrocard isn't just losing share, it's losing absolute riders. The transition is steady and monotonic; there's no sign of it stalling.

## Manhattan Never Sleeps (But the Bronx Does)

When you normalize each borough to its own peak hour, Manhattan stays lit through the overnight hours — its 1am ridership is still a meaningful fraction of its peak. The Bronx, by contrast, has an extremely sharp morning spike (7–8am) and drops to near-zero overnight. Brooklyn and Queens fall in between. Staten Island shows an odd 1am spike relative to its own peak, likely an artifact of very low absolute ridership making small numbers look large.

## Unlimited Cards Are for Commuters

Unlimited 30-Day and 7-Day cards produce textbook commuter curves: sharp twin peaks at 8am and 5pm with a midday valley. Full Fare riders (both Metrocard and OMNY) show a broader, flatter pattern — still commute-influenced but with more midday and evening activity. The unlimited curve is the purest signal of 9-to-5 commuting in the data.
