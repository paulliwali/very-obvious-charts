# Photographers vs. the Photo Lab — Takeaways

Everyone "knows" the smartphone killed professional photography. The obvious chart
says: it didn't. Photographers took a dent. The people who *developed the photos*
were erased.

Source: BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS), national files,
May 2003 – May 2025.

## By the numbers

| Occupation | 2007 (iPhone year) | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Photographers (27-4021) | 62,370 | 51,760 | **−17%** |
| Photo-lab & film processing (51-9131/9132 → 51-9151) | 71,780 | 4,800 | **−93%** |
| Film & video editors (27-4032) | 17,410 | 25,610 | **+47%** |
| Audio & video technicians (27-4011) | 40,360 | 70,230 | **+74%** |
| Camera operators, TV/video/film (27-4031) | 19,990 | 21,550 | **+8%** |
| All US jobs | 134.4M | 155.5M | +16% |

2007 was the all-time peak for photographers in this series. It has never been
matched since — but it also never collapsed.

## What actually happened

1. **The lab died, not the trade.** Developing film was the labor-intensive half
   of photography, and it was pure process work. Digital removed the process, so
   the process workers went with it. 71,780 → 4,800 in eighteen years.
2. **Photographers held.** −17% in raw headcount over a period when total US
   employment grew 16%. Per 100,000 jobs, photographers went from 46.4 to 33.3 —
   a real relative decline, but nothing like extinction. People still pay for
   weddings, headshots, real estate listings, and product shots. A phone in every
   pocket did not make anyone their own wedding photographer.
3. **The 2021 trough is COVID, not the iPhone.** Photographers bottomed at 38,420
   in 2021 — events and portraits stopped — then recovered to ~52,000 by 2023 and
   have held there. The smartphone-era trend line is a slow slide, not a cliff.
4. **The money moved to motion.** Film & video editors +47%, A/V technicians +74%.
   The same phones that stopped people printing photos started them watching
   video, and that work is counted in different occupation codes.

## Caveat worth stating

OEWS counts **wage-and-salary jobs only**. Photography is the most self-employed
trade on this chart: BLS's Occupational Outlook Handbook puts total photographer
jobs at **151,200 in 2024 — 66% of them self-employed** — against the 51,230
payroll jobs OEWS counts for the same year, a factor of three. So read this chart
as "photography *payroll* jobs." The same exclusion applies to every line on it,
and the shape of the story — labs annihilated, photographers dented — is what
survives.

## The actual takeaway

The smartphone didn't put photographers out of work. It put the *darkroom* out of
work. Automation eats the process, not the craft.
